## Character production

1. Generate head, hair and body separately when useful; retain consistent design,
   pose, scale and reference views. Inspect their joins, clearance and silhouette.
2. Inspect real geometry for tears, intersections, duplicate faces and suspected
   debris. Preserve originals and intentional openings, lashes, clothing and eyes.
   Use the requested Blender MCP against a verified, preferably isolated scene.
3. Deliver game LOW-POLY topology, with PC and mobile LOD/budget profiles. Count
   evaluated triangles including eyes/lashes/outline passes, not only authoring
   quads. Use economical eyelid/lip loops; do not add a dense facial grid.
   Smoothing old triangles, a quad percentage or a pretty render is not success.
   Inspect front, three-quarter, side and close-up native wireframes and clay.
4. Make UVs human-paintable: recognizable front face and back scalp, each ear
   separate, meaningful hair sections and garment panels, deliberate seams,
   orientation, padding and density. These are region goals, not mandatory disks.
   Diagnose stair-stepped seams, faceted geometry and render aliasing separately.
5. Inspect actual eye whites behind the iris; preserve lids, lashes and iris
   visibility. Added sclera does not establish blink/gaze or a finished eye rig.
6. Bake high-detail sources onto the low-poly UVs: real base-color and tangent
   normal outputs, projection/cage checks, padding, final triangulation, tangent
   convention and reloaded native comparison. PC/mobile need texture tiers;
   AO/masks are optional separate NPR controls. Normal maps cannot fix silhouettes.
   Reconstruct NPR textures from approved registered views and masks after
   topology/UV approval. Verify the requested image model/tool is available;
   names such as Image2.5/GPT imagine do not establish an API. Keep original UVs
   as reference; use seam-aware baking/projection, visibility and coverage checks.
   Do not substitute generated mockups for Blender/Unreal result screenshots.
7. Automate rigging, weight binding/copying and hair/cloth chains with explicit
   skeleton profiles, masks and deformation checks. Dynamics need collision and
   native runtime tests; bones alone are not simulation. Preserve skin/morph data.
   A creator-supplied fast path is: separately generated head/hair/body →
   calibrated neutral-pose assembly → optional external humanoid rig guide →
   weight transfer from an actually compatible, manually painted base →
   dedicated hair/garment chains. Treat it as a testable route, not an automatic
   service or evidence of finished skinning. A rigid head/hair attachment may be
   a reversible first pose test, never proof of strand motion. Keep VRM humanoid,
   source rig and each Unreal skeleton/export profile distinct.
8. Use blendable morphs only with valid topology/order correspondence. Generated
   expression heads may use discrete replacement with a tested selector; hiding
   shrunken heads is not universal export support or combinable expressions.

   Names alone do not prove rest pose, hierarchy or animation compatibility.
